title: The NIST AI Risk Management Framework Explained

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) is a voluntary framework published by the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology to help organizations identify, assess, and manage risks from AI systems throughout their lifecycle.

## Why a Voluntary Framework Matters

Unlike the EU AI Act, the NIST AI RMF imposes no legal obligations by itself, but it has become a de facto reference point for AI governance programs in the US and increasingly for procurement requirements, vendor risk assessments, and internal audit checklists — organizations often adopt it to demonstrate diligence even without a legal mandate to do so.

## The Four Core Functions

```text
GOVERN:  establish organizational culture, policies, and accountability for AI risk
MAP:     identify context, intended use, and risks specific to a given AI system
MEASURE: assess and track identified risks using appropriate metrics and methods
MANAGE:  prioritize and respond to risks, including mitigation and monitoring
```

`GOVERN` is treated as a cross-cutting function that should inform the other three throughout the AI lifecycle, rather than a one-time setup step — risk governance is meant to evolve as a system moves from design through deployment and monitoring.

## Trustworthiness Characteristics

The framework defines characteristics of trustworthy AI that `MAP` and `MEASURE` are meant to assess against: validity and reliability, safety, security and resilience, accountability and transparency, explainability and interpretability, privacy enhancement, and fairness with harmful bias managed. Treating these as distinct, individually measurable dimensions — rather than a single vague "responsible AI" checkbox — is one of the framework's more practically useful contributions.

## Practical Guidance

Use the AI RMF's `MAP` function early in a project to explicitly document intended use, likely misuse, and affected stakeholders before building — most AI harms trace back to a mismatch between intended and actual use that a structured mapping exercise would have surfaced. Treat the framework as a starting checklist to adapt to your organization's risk tolerance and sector-specific regulation (healthcare, finance, and hiring all carry additional legal obligations beyond what NIST's general framework covers), not as a complete compliance solution on its own.
